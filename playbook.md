# LinkedIn B2B SaaS Content Playbook

## Core Strategy
Post consistently with simple, clear messaging that focuses on one idea per post. Use personal experience and actionable insights to build trust.

---

## Recommended Approach

- Use short-form posts with one main idea (source: Justin Welsh, https://www.linkedin.com/in/justinwelsh/)
- Focus on value-driven content instead of storytelling fluff (source: Alex Hormozi, https://www.linkedin.com/in/alexhormozi/)
- Post consistently (3-5 times per week) to build audience (source: Amanda Natividad)

---

## Where Experts Disagree

### 1. Frequency vs Quality
- Justin Welsh: Post consistently, even if simple
- Alex Hormozi: Focus on high-value content over frequency  
**My take:** I agree with Justin Welsh because consistency builds habit and visibility faster.

### 2. Storytelling vs Direct Value
- Dave Gerhardt: Use storytelling to engage
- Alex Hormozi: Be direct and focus on value  
**My take:** I lean toward direct value because it is easier to scale and clearer.

### 3. Personal vs Educational Content
- Sahil Bloom: Personal storytelling
- Kieran Flanagan: Educational insights  
**My take:** A mix works best, but I prefer educational for B2B clarity.

---

## What I Rejected and Why

- Posting only personal stories: lacks actionable value for B2B audience  
- Overly long content: reduces readability and engagement  

---

## My Original Ideas

Create a weekly “framework post” series where each post teaches a repeatable system. This could work because audiences value consistency and structured learning.

---

## Weaknesses of This Playbook

- Based mostly on public content, not internal data  
- May not apply to all industries  
- Assumes audience is active on LinkedIn  

---

## Who I Would NOT Recommend Following and Why

Sahil Bloom: while engaging, his content is more general business and less focused on B2B SaaS strategy.
